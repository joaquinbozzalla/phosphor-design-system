01

Typography and Markdown

h1–h4, lead, paragraph, emphasis, inline code, rule.

Heading level one

The lead opens the article: IBM Plex Mono at 18px, highest-contrast color, with a barely-perceptible phosphor bloom. A single hook sentence, in sentence case — never title case.

Body copy runs in IBM Plex Mono Regular at 15px / 1.7. Long lines breathe over the green-black and the global scanline passes over them without disturbing the read. You can mark text in bold to raise contrast or in italics for a nuance, and drop in inline code that turns amber inside its inset box.

Heading level two

Sections are separated by typographic hierarchy, not by ornament. An internal link glows in primary green and underlines at a 4px offset on hover — brighten, never scale.

Heading level three

The third level keeps IBM Plex Mono but drops in weight. It subdivides a long argument without breaking the reading rhythm.

Fourth-level label

The h4 switches to uppercase mono with wide tracking: it reads as a panel label rather than a title. Useful for fields, steps and metadata inside the text.

The horizontal rule is a dotted phosphor-trace line: it separates movements of the article without the weight of a solid border.

Inline (internal / external) and link buttons.

The luminous green characteristic of CRTs; here, the system's primary color.
Printout
The light mode: green ink on parchment, no glow, like a printed session.
Scanline
A horizontal pattern at ~3% covering the whole page, giving screen texture.

Callouts, asides and footnotes

Four callout tones, margin aside, footnotes.

i
NoteContext worth highlighting without slowing the read. Blue border --info.
+
TipAn actionable recommendation. Uses primary green to signal something is worth doing.
!
CautionSomething that needs care but isn't critical. Amber --warning.
×
DangerA destructive or irreversible action. Red --danger, used sparingly.

The main text runs its normal course and can reference a footnote[1] exactly where it belongs. Margin notes, in contrast, live in a dotted-trace box[2].

[ ASIDE ] A tangential comment: complementary information that doesn't interrupt the main thread. The dotted border marks it as secondary content.
FOOTNOTES
  1. Footnotes use amber mono numbering and link back and forth with the body.
  2. The block lives at the end of the article, separated by a dotted rule.
